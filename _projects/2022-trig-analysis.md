My contributions to this project consist of the research behind and analysis of the diaphragm pump and the discussion of turbomachine selection for cylinder block cooling. 

After learning of the relatively small pressure drop at the carburetor throat from my groupmate, I decided to look further into the fuel system to investigate how fuel is reliably pulled into the carburetor regardless of orientation, viscous losses, and very slow flow velocity in the carb at engine idle which further decreases the pressure difference in the carb throat. A diaphragm pump is used to do the heavy lifting in pulling gas through the fuel system. It uses oscillating crankcase pressure to suck gas into a pre-carburetor metering chamber. From this metering chamber, the pressure drop in the carburetor can consistenly source gas, achieving the proper stoichiometric ratio. 

I also led a quick discussion on specific speed and the choice of a centrifugal blower over a possibly more efficient axial-flow turbomachine.
